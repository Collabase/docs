Comments keep the discussion about a page next to the text it is about. Mentions bring the right person into it. This page shows how to comment on a passage, how mentions notify people, and how a to-do in a page becomes a task for someone.

## Comment on a passage {#inline-comments}

An inline comment is attached to the text you select. The text is highlighted, and the discussion opens next to its block.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the text">
    Open the page and select the words the comment is about. You do not need to click **Edit**: a floating toolbar appears above the selection, and in a page you only read it offers **Comment** alone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the comment">
    Click **Comment** in the floating toolbar, type your comment and send it with **Enter** or the arrow button. Type `@` to mention someone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Continue the discussion">
    A number next to the block shows how many discussions it holds. Click the highlighted text or the number to open them. Type in **Reply...** to answer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You need the **Write comments** permission in the Space, see [Permissions](/concepts/permissions). Guests do not see **Comment** and cannot reply.

A comment is saved on its own. It does not change the page, does not appear in the [version history](/docs/version-history) and does not need **Save changes**. Everyone who opens the page sees it, also when the page is open in live collaboration, see [Live collaboration](/docs/collaboration).

### Work with a discussion {#discussions}

Hover over a comment you wrote to see its actions.

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Resolve** (check mark on the first comment of a discussion) | Closes the discussion and removes its highlight from the text |
| **Edit comment** | Changes the text of your comment. The comment then shows **(edited)** |
| **Delete comment** | Removes your comment. Deleting the first comment of a discussion removes the whole discussion |

### Who sees the highlight {#versions}

A comment belongs to the text it was written on. People who can edit the page see and comment on the draft. Everyone else sees the published text. A comment written on the draft appears for readers as soon as the draft is published, and only if the commented text is part of it.

If the commented text is edited, the highlight follows it as long as the words are still there. When the text is removed, the discussion is no longer shown on the page.

### When a comment cannot be saved {#troubleshooting}

If the connection or the server fails, the comment stays on your screen with **The comment could not be saved.** and a **Try again** button. Sending it again does not create a second comment, also when the first attempt did arrive. Changes to a comment you already sent, resolving and deleting show **The change could not be saved. Please try again.** and keep the earlier state.

An open page checks for new comments every 30 seconds while its tab is visible, and again when you return to the tab. Comments of other people show up without reloading the page.

## Mention a colleague {#mentions}

Type `@` followed by a name, in the page text or in a comment, and pick the person from the list. The mention shows as a name chip.

| Where you mention | What the person gets |
|---|---|
| **In a comment or a reply** | A notification "mentioned you in a discussion" in the notification center, and an email in their own language. The notification opens the page at that discussion. |
| **In a to-do item** | A notification **Task Assigned**, see [Assign a to-do](#to-dos) |
| **In the page text** | No notification. The mention is a link to the person only. |

When you edit a comment and add a mention, only the person you added is notified. A person who cannot open the page is shown greyed out as **No access** in the list and cannot be picked. A project guest who cannot read the page is not notified, even when mentioned.

Beyond mentions, a comment also notifies the author of the page, and a reply notifies everyone who wrote in the discussion, see [Notifications](/concepts/notifications#triggers).

## Assign a to-do to a person {#to-dos}

A to-do list item becomes a task as soon as it mentions a person. The first person mentioned in the item is its assignee.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a to-do list">
    In edit mode, type `/to-do` and press **Enter**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the task and mention the assignee">
    Write the task, then type `@` and pick the person, for example "Send the offer to the customer @Anna Muster".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the page">
    The task is created with the next save. The assignee receives the notification **Task Assigned** with a link to the page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Ticking the check box marks the task as done with the next save. Removing the item from the page removes the task.
