A Space is a self-contained area with its own members, modules and content. Think of it as a department, a product area or a team. One Collabase instance holds as many Spaces as you need.

Spaces are separate from each other. Content, members and settings of one Space do not affect any other. This makes a Space a reliable boundary for access: you can give a contractor access to one Space without exposing anything else.

## What a Space contains {#modules}

Each Space has its own set of modules. They decide which entries the Space shows in the left rail.

| Module | What it provides |
|---|---|
| **Documents** | Collabase Docs: pages, whiteboards and folders in a page tree. See [Collabase Docs](/docs/overview). |
| **Projects** | Work items, boards, timelines and budgets. See [Projects](/projects/overview). |
| **Test management** | Test cases, test runs and QA cycles. See [Test Management](/test-management/overview). |
| **Risks** | A risk register for the Space, with probability against impact |

A new Space starts with **Documents** and **Projects** switched on. Apps such as Studio, Registry, Time Tracking and Intranet are not part of a single Space; they work across all Spaces.

## Create a Space {#create}

Every user of the instance can create a Space, except project guests.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the space switcher">
    Click the space switcher at the top of the screen, then **Create Space**. The dialog **Create New Space** opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the Space">
    Enter the **Space Name**, for example "Engineering". Collabase derives the address of the Space from the name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the owner">
    By default you are the **Space owner**. Administrators can pick another user. The owner has full administrative access to the Space.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the modules">
    Under **Space Functions**, switch on the modules the Space needs. You can change them later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create it">
    Click **Build Space**. The Space opens, and you can [invite members](/getting-started/invite-members).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The address of a Space {#address}

Every Space has a short identifier, the slug, that appears in the address of everything in it:

```text
https://your-collabase-domain/{slug}
https://your-collabase-domain/{slug}/{page-id}
https://your-collabase-domain/{slug}/projects
```

The slug is derived from the name when the Space is created. If another Space already uses it, Collabase adds a number, for example `engineering-2`. Renaming the Space later does not change the slug, so existing links keep working.

## The Space home {#home}

Opening a Space shows its home page. It has a banner with the Space name and a grid of gadgets, for example a chart of page edits and creations in the last 7 days, the recently updated pages, quick actions and the people of the Space.

## Space roles {#roles}

Every member of a Space has a role. What the roles **Manager**, **Member** and **Viewer** may do is defined by the Space's <Tooltip tip="A reusable template that defines what Managers, Members and Viewers can do in a Space." cta="Glossary" href="/glossary#permission-scheme">Permission Scheme</Tooltip>. The **Space Admin** role manages the Space itself, and the Space owner always has full access.

See [Permissions](/concepts/permissions#space-roles) for what each role may do, and [Space settings](/concepts/space-settings) for adding members and groups.

## Turn modules on or off {#turn-modules-on-or-off}

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the space settings">
    Open the Space and go to its settings. You need the right to manage the Space's settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Features">
    Click **Features** in the **Space** group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch modules">
    Turn modules on or off. The message **Space features updated** confirms the change.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Turning a module off hides it in the Space. Its content stays and is back when you turn the module on again.

## Open a Space to everyone {#visibility}

By default, only members can read a Space. To open it to the whole organization:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the visibility settings">
    In the space settings, click **Visibility** in the **Access** group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it public">
    Turn on **Make space public**. Every signed-in user can then read pages in the Space without being a member.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A public Space can also show its blog posts as news in the Intranet. The same section has **Allow viewer suggestions**, which lets people without edit access propose changes, see [Suggestions](/docs/suggestions).

## Delete a Space {#delete}

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the danger zone">
    In the space settings, click **General** and scroll to **Danger Zone**. Under **Delete this space**, click **Delete**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm with the name">
    The dialog asks you to type the space name. Type it exactly and click **Delete space**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Deleting needs the right to archive the Space, which Space administrators have.

<Warning>
  Treat the deletion as final: the dialog states that the Space and all its projects, work items, pages and files are deleted and cannot be recovered. The Space first moves to the trash of the instance, where an administrator can still restore it. Ask your administrator at once if you deleted a Space by mistake.
</Warning>

## Spaces and projects {#spaces-vs-projects}

| | Space | Project |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | A top-level area for a department, a product area or a team | A structured initiative with work items, milestones and a board |
| **Members** | Managed per Space | Come from the Space, with optional project roles |
| **Contains** | Pages, projects, test management and risks | Work items, sprints, boards |
| **Address** | `/{slug}` | `/{slug}/projects/{project}` |

A Space usually holds several projects. A "Product" Space, for example, can hold one project per release.
