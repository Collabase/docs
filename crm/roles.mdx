The CRM decides what a person sees through **roles**. A role lists, for each object type, which of five rights it gives. A person holds the rights of every role they have, directly or through a group. A person without a role sees nothing, and the app answers "not found".

Roles and settings are for people who hold the right to **administer the CRM**. For everyone else both pages answer "not found". A system administrator without that right has no access either: the global administrator role opens nothing inside the CRM. The person who first opens the CRM as system administrator becomes CRM admin.

You find both in the system administration under **CRM**: the settings at `/admin/crm/settings` and the roles at `/admin/crm/roles`. Each page links to the other.

## Roles

A new CRM has five shipped roles. You can change their rights, field rules and members, but not their names, and you cannot delete them.

| Role | Sees | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| **CRM admin** | Everything | Everything, and administers the CRM |
| **Sales** | Everything | Everything, including opportunities |
| **Staff** | Companies, people, notes, tasks, lists | Create and change companies, people, notes, tasks and lists, but not delete them and not opportunities |
| **Management** | Everything | Nothing; can export |
| **Readers** | Companies, people, notes, tasks, lists | Nothing |

<Note>
Only the **CRM admin** role starts with a member, the person who activated the CRM. The other roles start empty.
</Note>

### Create a role

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a role">
    Click **New role** and enter a name. Names are unique without regard to upper or lower case.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the rights">
    Tick the rights per object type in the matrix. A new role has none.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add members">
    Add people or whole groups under **Members**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. The change applies from the next request of every member.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Rights per object type

| Right | Gives |
|---|---|
| **See** | The type in the sidebar, the search, the timeline and the counts, and its records |
| **Create** | New records of the type |
| **Change** | Edit records of the type |
| **Delete** | Delete records of the type |
| **Export** | Export records of the type |

Every right needs **See**. Ticking another right ticks **See** for you, and clearing **See** clears the rest. Opportunities are an ordinary type: remove **See** on them from a role and its members never meet an opportunity, in the search, the timeline, an export or a notification.

### Field rules

For every type a role can see, you can hide a field or make it read only. A field without a rule follows the right on its type. Besides the fields of the type you can rule the **Owner**, the **Domain**, the **Email addresses** and the **Phone numbers**.

If a person holds several roles, the most permissive rule wins: a field is hidden only if every role that lets the person see the type hides it.

### Special rights

| Right | Gives |
|---|---|
| **Administer the CRM** | Roles, objects, fields, pipelines and settings |
| **Connect a mailbox** | Connecting their own mailbox and calendar |
| **Data protection office** | Answering data protection requests, without access to private notes |

The last person who holds **Administer the CRM** cannot lose it. The CRM refuses a change that would remove it, whether you delete the role, take the right away, remove the member or the group, and says so.

### Members

A member is one person or one group. Only active internal people can hold a role: service accounts, guests and customers cannot, even through a group. Members load a page at a time. Adding someone who already holds the role changes nothing.

### Delete a role

Delete a role you created with **Delete**. Its members lose its rights at their next request. A shipped role cannot be deleted.

### Why does someone not see a record?

Under **Why does someone not see a record?** pick a person and paste the address of the record. The answer is one sentence:

- the person has no access to the CRM,
- their roles give no right to see this record,
- the record is in the trash,
- or they see the record, but some fields are hidden for them, and which.

The answer is the same for a record that does not exist and for one in a type the person may not read.

## Settings

The settings hold the values two installations may need differently.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Retention in months** | How long mail and appointments stay in the CRM. Collabase deletes older ones once a day. From 1 to 120 months. |
| **Contacts from mail** | The most any mailbox may create: none, from sent mail, or from sent and received mail. A mailbox owner can choose less. |
| **Reason required for lost deals** | Moving an opportunity to a lost stage asks for the reason. |
| **Logo service** | An address with `{domain}` in it, such as `https://logos.example.com/{domain}`. The CRM asks it for a company logo by the company's domain, only when the company's own site has none. Only the domain is sent. Leave it empty to never contact a third party. |
| **Mail and calendar apps** | Client ID and client secret of the Google and Microsoft apps of this installation. |

A client secret is write only. After you save it the page shows only that one is set. To replace it, type a new one. To remove it, click **Remove** and save.

## Audit

Every change to a role, a membership or a setting is written to the audit log. A role entry holds the role, the rights that changed and the ids of members added or removed, never their names. A settings entry holds the names of the keys that changed, never a value and never a secret.
